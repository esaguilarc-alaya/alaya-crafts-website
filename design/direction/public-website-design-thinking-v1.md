# Alaya Crafts Public Website

## Design-thinking direction — V1

**Status:** Proposed direction for review before visual design  
**Purpose:** Establish the public-site narrative, information architecture, visual concept, and homepage composition  
**Desired first reaction:** “There is an interesting idea here. I want to understand more.”

---

## 1. Interpretation: what Alaya Crafts has become

Alaya Crafts is becoming a company organized around a point of view: **capability is the enduring asset**.

It is not primarily a consultancy with software attached, nor a software company using philosophy as brand decoration. It is an emerging ecosystem of knowledge, technology, products, and services built around the cultivation of capability in people and organizations.

Its unifying sequence is:

**Curiosity → Understanding → Learning → Contribution → Reflection → renewed Curiosity**

Within that system:

- **Capability** is what endures.
- **Knowledge** preserves and makes capability understandable.
- **Technology** helps that understanding become usable, repeatable, and connected.
- **Crafts** are the practices through which capability keeps developing.
- **CIE Professional** is the first concrete product expression, not the whole company.
- Organizational work is another expression of the same idea, not a conventional service catalogue.

The public site should therefore feel like the entrance to a body of work that is still unfolding: coherent enough to trust, open enough to invite exploration, and honest about what exists today.

## 2. The website’s primary job

The site’s first job is **orientation, not conversion**.

It should help a thoughtful first-time visitor move from an intriguing belief to a grounded understanding:

1. Alaya begins with curiosity.
2. People and organizations create capability continuously, but much of it remains fragmented, implicit, or difficult to preserve.
3. Alaya treats capability—not technology, software, or transformation activity—as the enduring asset.
4. Alaya is building a connected body of intelligence, knowledge, technology, products, and services around that idea.
5. CIE Professional is where this idea can already be encountered most concretely.
6. Organizational work applies the same philosophy at a different scale.

Only after this orientation should the site offer paths forward. Those paths should be plural and contextual rather than dominated by “Book a call.”

## 3. V1 audiences

### Primary: curious professionals

People trying to understand, grow, or express their professional capability. They need a recognizable doorway into CIE Professional without first learning Alaya’s internal architecture.

### Primary: thoughtful organizational leaders

Leaders responsible for strategy, execution, systems, technology, knowledge, or organizational capability. They may enter through a practical problem, but should leave understanding that Alaya’s view is deeper than a consulting engagement.

### Secondary: collaborators and future contributors

Designers, researchers, technologists, practitioners, and potential partners who recognize the body of work and may want to contribute to it.

### Secondary: idea-led readers

People who arrive through an essay, shared concept, or recommendation. For them, the site must support understanding without forcing an immediate commercial path.

The V1 homepage should not split these audiences too early. The philosophy is the shared entrance; product and organizational paths diverge only after the central idea is understood.

## 4. Proposed narrative arc

### Movement 1 — Invite curiosity

Open with restraint. Establish an atmosphere and one clear thought, then provide enough grounding to prevent the opening from becoming vague or mystical.

### Movement 2 — Name the overlooked problem

People and organizations create capability through work, learning, decisions, evidence, and contribution. Yet that capability is often scattered across systems, documents, roles, memories, and moments. Tools change; much of what was learned disappears with them.

### Movement 3 — Reveal the Alaya idea

Make the philosophical triad the clearest conceptual moment on the page:

> Capability is the enduring asset.  
> Knowledge preserves it.  
> Technology amplifies it.

The third line must be treated carefully in supporting copy so “amplifies” does not collapse into familiar AI rhetoric. The design should show technology as infrastructure beneath the idea, not as protagonist.

### Movement 4 — Show capability as living practice

Introduce the five-part cycle as movement rather than doctrine. Curiosity does not begin a funnel; it begins a recurring practice.

### Movement 5 — Give the idea form

Reveal what Alaya is building in plain public language. Internal architecture may guide the model, but visitors should meet concepts before acronyms.

### Movement 6 — Demonstrate different scales

Show that the same idea can operate for a professional, a team, and an organization. Be explicit that these expressions are at different stages of maturity.

### Movement 7 — Make it concrete

Present CIE Professional as the first living expression of the larger philosophy. Present organizational work as a grounded pathway, not a menu of service pillars.

### Movement 8 — Leave the field open

Close with “Technology evolves. Crafts endure.” and quiet, distinct next paths: explore the product, understand the organizational work, or continue into the ideas.

## 5. Minimum V1 information architecture

### Home

**Visitor question:** What is Alaya Crafts, why does it exist, and where can I enter?

The homepage carries the complete introductory argument. It should be sufficient for a first encounter.

### The Alaya Idea

**Visitor question:** What does Alaya mean by capability, knowledge, technology, and crafts?

This combines philosophy, company story, and the capability practice. A separate generic “About” page is unnecessary in V1 if this page carries the reason Alaya exists and the human history behind it.

### CIE Professional

**Visitor question:** What is the first product, who is it for, and what experience does it create?

This page can inherit more directly from the established CIE product language while remaining a public product story rather than a simulated application screen.

### For Organizations

**Visitor question:** How does Alaya’s capability philosophy apply to organizational work, and what can we explore together now?

Avoid a services grid. Organize the page around organizational conditions, the Alaya approach, and a small number of honest forms of engagement once those are confirmed.

### Ideas

**Visitor question:** Can I follow and understand the body of thinking before becoming a customer?

Include only if there is enough real material to sustain it. Otherwise reserve the navigation label and defer the page rather than launch an empty “Insights” shell.

### V1 navigation

**Alaya Crafts** · The Alaya Idea · CIE Professional · For Organizations · Ideas (when ready)

Primary navigation should not include both “About” and “Philosophy.” It should also avoid a persistent high-emphasis sales button. A restrained “Start a conversation” text action may appear once an actual contact path is defined.

## 6. What to preserve directly from Foundations

- The belief: **Everything starts with curiosity.**
- The central assertion: **Capability is the enduring asset.**
- The relationship between capability, knowledge, and technology.
- The five-part capability practice and its recursive return to curiosity.
- **Technology evolves. Crafts endure.** as a strong closing thought.
- Warm paper and ivory surfaces; charcoal rather than pure black.
- Restrained pine, forest, and amber palette.
- Cormorant Garamond for expressive editorial moments and Montserrat for functional reading/navigation.
- Fine rules, generous whitespace, restrained hierarchy, and editorial pacing.
- Organic flow, contour, mountain, and accumulated-landscape motifs.
- A visual atmosphere closer to a thoughtful publication, field notebook, workshop, or body of knowledge than enterprise software.

## 7. What to transform for the web

- Turn the static capability cycle into a gentle, user-controlled sequence that reveals one practice at a time and completes the loop without auto-playing spectacle.
- Translate diagrams into narrative transitions. The visitor should understand relationships through order, proximity, and motion before seeing labels such as CIE or AlayaFlows.
- Use scale changes and sectional pacing to create discovery, rather than placing every foundation statement at equal visual weight.
- Let contour and flow lines connect sections as a quiet continuity device, not as decorative wallpaper.
- Allow editorial typography to become more spatial and responsive while preserving comfortable reading widths.
- Use interaction to disclose depth: a visitor can pause on capability, knowledge, technology, or each craft to reveal a concise explanation.
- Translate the CIE product family resemblance into tone and behavior: calm states, precise language, progressive disclosure, accessible focus, and no unsupported certainty.
- If imagery is introduced later, favor tactile evidence of making, learning, work, and observation. Avoid stock boardrooms, handshake photography, floating dashboards, heroic founders, and literal mountain scenery.

## 8. What should not appear publicly

- Internal architecture diagrams presented as the homepage explanation.
- Acronym-first language such as CIE, ACI, OKM, MCI, or Representation without a human explanation and a visitor need.
- Product or organizational expressions presented as available when they remain emerging.
- Claims about transformation, productivity, intelligence, ROI, proprietary advantage, or scale without evidence.
- A full consulting catalogue invented to make the company appear larger.
- Customer logos, testimonials, or metrics that do not yet have a real and permissioned basis.
- Detailed governance or epistemic mechanics belonging in product or technical material.
- A “technology stack” story. Technology supports the work; it is not the identity.
- Founder mythology. The history should explain the insight, not elevate the founder as the product.

## 9. Visual and interaction concept

### Concept: The Living Field

The website behaves like a body of knowledge gradually coming into view.

The visual field begins sparse: paper, language, one fine moving line. As the visitor proceeds, lines recur, overlap, and form relationships. By the time the ecosystem is revealed, the page has accumulated a quiet terrain of ideas—without becoming an infographic or dashboard.

This extends the strongest CIE principle: **knowledge can accumulate without the interface shouting more loudly**.

### Visual grammar

- **Surface:** warm paper with subtle tonal variation; not faux parchment.
- **Type:** expressive Cormorant headlines paired with precise Montserrat labels and body copy.
- **Color:** charcoal for language, pine for grounded ideas, lighter forest for connective material, amber for emergence and invitation—not alerts.
- **Lines:** hand-drawn or topographic in feeling, but controlled and sparse. They connect and accumulate rather than illustrate literal mountains.
- **Composition:** asymmetric editorial layouts, shifting measures, large breathing spaces, and occasional edge-to-edge conceptual fields.
- **Objects:** few cards. Prefer sections, annotations, marginalia, fine rules, and layered planes.

### Interaction grammar

- Motion is slow, local, and responsive to the visitor; no ambient motion that competes with reading.
- Hover and focus reveal supporting thoughts, but all essential meaning remains available without interaction.
- Scroll creates continuity, not a cinematic takeover.
- The capability cycle is operable by keyboard and touch and respects reduced-motion preferences.
- Links describe destinations: “Explore CIE Professional,” “See how Alaya works with organizations,” and “Read the Alaya idea.”
- Transitions should feel like following a thought, not entering a sales funnel.

### Family resemblance with CIE Professional

Preserve:

- low cognitive friction;
- conversational, evidence-aware language;
- epistemic humility and clear distinctions between established and emerging;
- paper, pine, amber, contours, and editorial typography;
- progressive disclosure;
- accessible, quiet interactions;
- the sense of an evolving connected landscape.

Do not copy:

- application shell, workspace navigation, product panels, status controls, form treatments, dashboards, or user-task composition;
- the Capability Landscape as a literal corporate hero graphic;
- product-specific semantics such as evidence states in a purely brand context.

The corporate site is the landscape seen from outside; CIE Professional is a place where someone works within it.

## 10. Contradictions and unresolved positioning questions

### A. “Everything starts with curiosity” is distinctive but not self-explanatory

It can open the site, but it needs immediate grounding in capability. Alone, it risks reading as poetic brand language applicable to a school, studio, or research lab.

**Recommendation:** Pair it with a plain-spoken orientation line within the first viewport.

### B. “Technology amplifies it” is canonical but rhetorically crowded

“Amplify” has become common AI language. Alaya means something more precise: technology helps knowledge become usable, connected, and repeatable without becoming the purpose.

**Recommendation:** Preserve the canonical triad, then clarify it through concrete supporting language rather than replacing it prematurely.

### C. The ecosystem is conceptually mature but unevenly available

CIE Professional is concrete. CIE Teams, CIE Organization, and parts of AlayaFlows may be emerging. A system diagram can accidentally imply a mature suite.

**Recommendation:** Design explicit maturity language—“available,” “in development,” and “an emerging direction”—and never use a uniform product-card grid.

### D. Organizational work needs a real present-tense offer

“Reducing friction between strategy and execution” remains a useful practical entry, but it does not define the whole company. The site still needs to say what an organization can actually do with Alaya now.

**Decision needed before final copy:** Confirm the two or three real engagement forms currently available.

### E. The role of “Crafts” needs enough meaning to justify the name

The five practices are compelling, but visitors may not naturally understand why the company is called Alaya Crafts.

**Recommendation:** Let the capability-practice section quietly answer this. Avoid a defensive etymology section on the homepage.

### F. The human origin matters, but the right depth is unresolved

Years of work across technology, engineering, organizations, execution, and knowledge explain why the capability thesis emerged. Too little history makes Alaya feel abstract; too much turns it into a founder consultancy.

**Recommendation:** Add a short origin passage on “The Alaya Idea” page and one sentence on the homepage. Defer a full company timeline.

### G. The immediate actions are not yet equally defined

“Explore CIE Professional” can be concrete. “For Organizations” can lead to a clear explanation. “Ideas” depends on available material. “Start a conversation” depends on a real contact pathway.

**Decision needed before launch:** Confirm which actions have real destinations in V1.

## 11. Homepage composition, section by section

### 0. Header — A quiet orientation bar

**Content:** Alaya Crafts wordmark; The Alaya Idea; CIE Professional; For Organizations; Ideas when ready.  
**Behavior:** Transparent or paper-toned, becoming lightly bounded after scroll. No high-emphasis “Book a call” button.  
**Purpose:** Make the ecosystem navigable without announcing corporate scale.

### 1. Hero — Curiosity, grounded

**Eyebrow:** Alaya Crafts  
**Headline:** Everything starts with curiosity.  
**Grounding line, working direction:** Alaya Crafts cultivates lasting capability through knowledge, technology, products, and thoughtful practice.  
**Primary path:** Explore the idea  
**Secondary path:** Meet CIE Professional

**Visual behavior:** A single fine contour enters the field and changes gently as the visitor moves or scrolls. The viewport remains mostly paper. Avoid a dark background, scenic mountain hero, product screenshot, or abstract AI glow.

### 2. Observation — What disappears when the work moves on

**Purpose:** Name the human and organizational problem without manufacturing urgency.

**Working copy idea:** People and organizations create capability every day—through experience, learning, decisions, evidence, and contribution. Much of it remains scattered, difficult to understand, and easy to lose.

**Composition:** Two related editorial columns—people and organizations—joined by a faint line, not two market-segment cards.

### 3. Thesis — The enduring asset

**Primary statement:** Capability is the enduring asset.  
**Supporting sequence:** Knowledge preserves it. Technology amplifies it.

**Purpose:** Deliver the conceptual center of the site.

**Composition:** A deliberate typographic shift. Each statement enters the same field and remains visible, building a relationship rather than replacing the previous line.

### 4. Practice — Capability stays alive through practice

**Content:** Curiosity → Understanding → Learning → Contribution → Reflection → renewed Curiosity.

**Purpose:** Prevent “capability” from sounding like a static inventory of skills.

**Interaction:** A calm circular or meandering sequence. Selecting a practice reveals one plain-spoken sentence and subtly changes the shared flow line. No autoplay, spinning wheel, or gamified progress.

### 5. Ecosystem — What Alaya is building

**Public-facing sequence:**

1. Make capability understandable.
2. Preserve what is learned as durable knowledge.
3. Make that knowledge usable through thoughtful technology.
4. Bring it to life through products and organizational practice.

**Purpose:** Explain the architecture without forcing visitors to memorize it.

**Secondary disclosure:** CIE and AlayaFlows may be named here only after their role is clear in ordinary language.

**Composition:** One connected field with annotated layers, not four equal feature cards.

### 6. Scales — One idea, different contexts

**Professional:** The most concrete expression today.  
**Teams:** An emerging scale of the same capability idea.  
**Organizations:** Capability understood and cultivated across systems of work.

**Purpose:** Establish scope without pretending every scale is a finished product.

**Composition:** A widening field or nested relationship. Avoid three pricing-like cards. Use explicit maturity labels.

### 7. CIE Professional — The idea becomes an experience

**Working headline:** Understand the capability your work has created.  
**Supporting idea:** CIE Professional helps a person build an evidence-grounded professional understanding that can grow through experience, reflection, objectives, and contribution.  
**Action:** Explore CIE Professional

**Visual direction:** An editorial glimpse of the product’s language and evolving landscape—not a dashboard screenshot wall. Show how the experience thinks and feels.

### 8. Organizations — A practical doorway

**Working headline:** Capability connects strategy to the work that carries it.  
**Supporting idea:** Alaya works with organizations where strategy, operating systems, knowledge, technology, and human capability need to become more coherent.

**Action:** How Alaya works with organizations

**Purpose:** Make present organizational work legible without turning the homepage into consulting marketing.

**Composition:** One substantial passage plus a small set of real organizational questions. No service-pillar grid until actual offers are confirmed.

### 9. Origin — Why this body of work exists

**Content:** One short passage connecting more than two decades of work across technology, engineering, leadership, product thinking, execution, and organizations to the realization that capability is the more enduring thread.

**Purpose:** Make Alaya human and earned without centering a founder biography.

**Action:** Read the Alaya idea

### 10. Crafts — The recurring practices

**Content:** Curiosity. Understanding. Learning. Contribution. Reflection.

**Purpose:** Let the company name resolve with quiet inevitability near the end.

**Composition:** Five words distributed as a connected cadence. This is a signature, not a second explanation of the earlier cycle.

### 11. Closing — An open invitation

**Headline:** Technology evolves. Crafts endure.  
**Supporting line:** Choose the part of the idea that is most useful to you now.  
**Paths:** Explore CIE Professional · For Organizations · Follow the ideas

**Purpose:** End with agency and curiosity rather than pressure.

### 12. Footer — Small and factual

Include only real destinations, company/legal information, and a restrained contact route. Avoid a newsletter prompt unless a publication cadence exists.

## 12. Recommended next design step

Proceed to a low-fidelity responsive homepage composition focused on:

1. first-viewport balance between poetry and clarity;
2. the transition from observation to thesis;
3. the interactive capability-practice sequence;
4. the ecosystem reveal without acronym overload;
5. honest maturity treatment across Professional, Teams, and Organization;
6. clear visual distinction between the corporate story and the CIE product doorway.

Do not produce a polished style system or final marketing copy yet. The next artifact should test whether a stranger can understand the sequence with minimal explanation.
