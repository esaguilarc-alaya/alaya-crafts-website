# Slice 02 — For Organizations: Resolved Decisions (2026-08-26)

**Status:** Answers to the "Required design decisions" in `02-for-organizations.md`. Read alongside
the original slice brief — this document resolves its open questions, it doesn't replace it. The
original's excluded list, acceptance criteria, and stop condition all still apply.

## 1. What engagement exists today

**None.** There is no current organizational engagement to describe. This changes the page's job
more than it might first appear: the challenge was never "how do we describe our services without
sounding like consulting theater" — there are no services yet to over-describe. The page's job is
closer to what the CIE Professional page already does: state a genuine thesis, ground it in a real
example of the problem, and close with an honest, low-key invitation — not a pitch, because nothing
is being sold yet.

**Implication for copy:** nothing on the page should imply an active organizational practice through
vague present-tense language ("we work with organizations to..."). Say plainly that this is a
direction grounded in the same principle as CIE Professional, not yet a built organizational product.

## 2. The strategy/capability line

Do not use "we help organizations reduce friction between strategy and execution" — it now reads as
an empty slogan the market has worn out. The idea survives without the phrase.

**Use instead**, as the page's actual thesis (not merely supporting language):

> Strategy gives capability direction. Capability turns strategy into action.

Treat this couplet as the header-level thesis. If a plainer restatement is needed underneath it,
something like: *strategy sets direction, but organizations often can't see whether the capability to
carry it out actually exists, is fragmented, or was never there* — same ground the old line was
gesturing at, without the borrowed phrase.

**Sequencing note:** open the page with the CIE Professional lineage (see #7 below) before this
couplet, not after. The couplet lands harder once the reader already knows it's an extension of a
real, evidence-based method, not a new claim invented for this page.

## 3. CIE Teams

Teams is a usage of CIE Organizations, not a separate offering. Nothing built. Do not give it its own
section or pillar. At most, a passing acknowledgment that capability shows up at team scale too,
folded into the organizational narrative — never presented as an available or near-available product.

## 4. Organizational example

Use the CDN-provider network-operations example, anonymized:

> A network operations team at a CDN provider — TAC engineers adapting to new tooling and domain
> demands. Leadership agreed to pursue training, but the decision was made without a clear picture of
> what the engineers actually knew — leadership overstated some engineers' real depth and
> underestimated others', around the same training decision.

Anonymize as "a CDN provider" or "a global infrastructure company" rather than naming the company.
This example is the page's strongest asset — concrete, true, and sharper than a generic "leaders
don't know what their people can do" claim, because the miscalibration runs in *both* directions at
once. Build the "what is difficult to see" section (brief section 3) around this example rather than
inventing abstract categories.

## 5. Contact — two distinct mechanisms, not one

CIE Organization is a future solution, not a described engagement model — so contact should not be
written as if it explains "how working together works." Two separate mechanisms:

- **General contact** — plain, direct, human. Exists site-wide, not specific to this page.
- **Organizations-page-specific invitation** — a distinct, closing element on this page only: an
  invitation for someone who recognizes the problem to share their experience and help shape CIE
  Organization before it launches. Something like: *"If this matches something you've seen, and
  you'd want to help shape it before launch, say so."* No promise of a callback, no implied pipeline,
  no diagnostic or intake form — just an open door. Keep it visually and narratively distinct from the
  general Contact mechanism; this is a "help us think" ask, not a "get in touch to discuss working
  together" ask, since the latter isn't yet honest to make.

## 6. Audience

**Primary reader: an operations or technical leader** — CIO, CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Ops. Two
reasons: the CDN example itself is a technical-capability miscalibration story, most legible to
someone who owns technical execution; and this audience matches Esteban's own voice and lived
credibility, where CIE Professional's HR-adjacency comes from its subject matter (hiring, interview
evaluation) rather than from addressing HR as its reader.

**Secondary reader: HR / L&D**, who often co-own the training/development decision the CDN example
turns on. Write the page's questions and voice for the operations leader first; let HR readers
recognize themselves in it rather than being addressed directly.

**Note on apparent inconsistency with CIE Professional:** CIE Professional's subject matter is
HR-adjacent because hiring is inherently HR-adjacent — but its reader is the individual candidate, not
HR. The Organizations page's subject matter (does an organization know what its people can actually
do) is closer to Esteban's own operational experience. Different primary readers across the two pages
is consistent with one shared philosophy, not a contradiction — worth stating this plainly if it comes
up, so it doesn't read as an accidental inconsistency.

## 7. CIE Organization's relationship to CIE Professional

Open the page by grounding CIE Organization's credibility in CIE Professional's already-real method,
before introducing the strategy/capability couplet. The page's core credibility problem is "nothing is
built yet for organizations" — anchoring explicitly to CIE Professional solves this honestly: not
"trust us, we have a new idea," but "the same evidence-over-assertion principle CIE Professional
applies to individual capability applies at organizational scale — the organizational instrument just
isn't built yet." This is honest about what doesn't exist while still giving the visitor something
real to trust: the method is proven in one direction already.

## Revised narrative order

1. **Opening — shared principle.** Ground CIE Organization in CIE Professional's method before making
   any organizational claim.
2. **Thesis.** Strategy gives capability direction. Capability turns strategy into action.
3. **The organizational condition.** Use the CDN example directly here, not as a later illustration —
   it should carry the section, not decorate it.
4. **What is difficult to see.** Fragmented, implicit, duplicated, or immobile capability — grounded in
   the same example's both-directions miscalibration.
5. **A different lens.** What changes once capability (not just headcount or process) is the unit of
   understanding.
6. **Questions Alaya explores.** Real organizational questions, not service pillars (per original
   brief).
7. **Technology's role.** Connects and extends understanding; never the protagonist.
8. **Human provenance.** The approach emerged from sustained work across technology, engineering,
   leadership — Esteban's actual background, understated.
9. **Two closing actions.** (a) General contact — plain, human, site-wide. (b) The organizations-page
   invitation to share experience ahead of launch — distinct, not a disguised lead form.
