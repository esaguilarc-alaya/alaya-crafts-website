# Alaya Crafts Website — Page Design Slices

These slices extend the approved homepage into three distinct public destinations. They are **design slices**, not production implementation slices.

## Sequence

1. **CIE Professional** — define the most concrete expression of Alaya first.
2. **For Organizations** — define the organizational doorway without inventing a consultancy catalogue.
3. **Ideas** — define how the body of thinking is published and explored without launching an empty “Insights” shell.

The order matters. CIE Professional provides concrete language for capability at the individual scale. For Organizations then expands the same philosophy honestly. Ideas can finally connect and deepen both without becoming generic content marketing.

## Shared design process

Each slice must complete:

1. page interpretation and primary visitor question;
2. narrative and minimum information architecture;
3. working copy sufficient to test composition;
4. low-fidelity desktop and mobile composition;
5. critical design review;
6. polished responsive design only after low-fidelity approval.

Production implementation, framework selection, deployment, analytics, forms, CMS behavior, and search are deferred until the relevant design decisions are approved.

## Shared guardrails

- Preserve family resemblance with the homepage and CIE Professional product without copying product UI.
- Explain concepts before relying on acronyms.
- Distinguish what exists, what is emerging, and what is only possible.
- Keep the Capability Landscape relational and conceptual—not a product map.
- Use orientation before conversion.
- Avoid service-card grids, SaaS feature grids, fake product screens, generic “Insights,” unsupported claims, and conversion pressure.
- Contact remains direct and human.
- Desktop and mobile must be designed together.

## Cross-slice coherence review

After all three slices are approved, review the complete public-site journey for:

- duplicated explanations;
- contradictory maturity language;
- navigation labels and return paths;
- consistent use of Capability Intelligence, CIE, and the Capability Landscape;
- whether each page answers a genuinely different visitor question;
- whether the homepage remains the primary orientation layer rather than a summary of every page.
